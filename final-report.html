What happened, plainly

A shorter ping wait met a scoring rule that can't tell "busy" from "no"

12 Aug4.2 cuts the ping wait from 90s to 60s.
More missesBusy or out-of-signal responders run out of time far more often.
Scored as a "no"Code gives a miss the same −0.12 as a turn-down. A yes earns only +0.08.
No way backScores never recover on their own, so a bad week sticks.
DroppedThe four sink in the ranking and stop being asked. Nobody is told.

01Origin story · finding the contradiction
The prompt I wroteModule 1 · #1
What's contradictory or missing, not just in the company documents, but across Rook's wiki, its database, and the files in my repo?

The wiki and database say a miss and a "no" are different. The code says they're the same.

The glossary records missed and turned down separately and the database keeps them apart. But offer.py sends every non-yes to record_declined. That one line is the root of 4.2's damage, and it had been sitting there since Wen's 2019 note.

for responder in routing.rank_for_callout(callout): answer = offer_to(responder, callout) if answer == TAKEN: history.record_accepted(responder) # +0.08 return responder history.record_declined(responder) # −0.12 for # "turned down" AND "missed"

04X-ray vision · reading the code
The prompt I wroteModule 4 · #5
what would have to happen for them to start getting work again.

Rolling back 4.2 wouldn't bring them back

The only thing that adds points is a yes (+0.08). There's no recovery over time and no reset. A responder at 0 needs about 10 yeses in a row to get back to a typical 0.8, and at Farlight's ~1 ping a week that's around 10 weeks, if she's asked at all.

Climbing back from 0, one yes at a time

Score after each consecutive yes (+0.08). Typical responders sit around 0.8–1.0.
